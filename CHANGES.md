# Changes

One row per change to the Skill, newest first: what changed, why, and what it replaces.

| Date | What changed | Why | Replaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-27 | Second edition (2.0.0). A system is looked at in two layers: a metaphysical layer, what the system is in its own terms, discussed as one inquiry into its things, acts and actors, and a physical layer, whatever carries it out, working by its own field's principles, which the Skill does not contain. In place of "sources of truth", the Skill asks of anything that matters how it comes to be and who may change it, and it does not appeal to "fact" or "truth". The controlled vocabulary is withdrawn and the Skill is written as inquiry, with fields' own words kept. `references/model.md` and `references/activities.md` are rewritten, and every case is read again | Applied to a software system, the first edition let a design treat each state as settled by whichever party had authority over it, the stance of systems that record a domain outside themselves, and look past the mechanisms that make each state; its controlled vocabulary drew writing toward a closed formal language | The first edition's three perspectives (logical, actor, implementation), its "Owner and source of truth" entry and its vocabulary |
| 2026-10-25 | First edition (1.0.0). A model of artificial systems under one shared governance, seen from logical, actor and implementation perspectives, whose actors organize vertically by delegation and horizontally by services, requests, allocation and support (`references/model.md`); a controlled vocabulary; eight activities (`references/activities.md`); and sixteen case studies of public systems (`references/cases/`). The judgment of the earlier Skill is carried into the model and the activities as heuristics with their reasons and limits | The earlier Skill was organized around design activities without a model of the system they act on | The earlier Skill `problem-and-system-design` (1.0.0), now retired |
