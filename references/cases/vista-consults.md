# VistA consults in the Veterans Health Administration

The system studied here is the way clinical services of the Veterans Health Administration (VHA) ask one another for work and learn the outcome, as VHA policy defines it and the consult package of VistA (the VA's electronic health record software) and its CPRS front end carry it. The governance is VHA's: a national directive, the facility directors, chiefs of staff and service chiefs it assigns duties to, and the local committee that oversees consults. Veterans are the stakeholders the work is done for. Community care clinicians outside VA, to whom consults are forwarded, are suppliers. The case rests on the Consult/Request Tracking User Manual (version 3.0, August 2024) and VHA Directive 1232 (22 November 2024).

## What a consult is

The directive defines a consult as "a request for service on behalf of a Veteran" and uses the word for VistA's "consult" and for the "referral" of the Oracle Health platform alike. A referring clinician orders it in CPRS; the receiving service gets an alert and a printed form; and the request, as the manual puts it, "acts as a depository of information about itself. It collects notes and keeps records on everything that happens to it." The consult is a shared record that both services act on, and its meaning lives in its statuses and actions.

The actions mark who does what. In the directive's definitions, Receive "automatically updates the status of the consult from Pending to Active"; the manual adds that this "puts your clinic on record as accepting responsibility for completing the consult". The receiving service may also forward the consult to another service ("not to a specific clinician"), schedule it, cancel or deny it, or complete it. The reply is a note written through the Text Integration Utilities (TIU). Saved unsigned, it puts the consult in Partial Results; signed, it completes the consult and alerts the referring clinician. A request that waits, one accepted, one with an appointment linked, one with a reply under way and one finished are therefore different observations, and the record keeps them apart. Intent, acceptance, progress and outcome each have a status or action of their own.

How the work is done is the receiving service's decision. The directive lets the receiving clinician answer a face-to-face request as an E-Consult and says the referring clinician "may request an E-Consult", while receiving clinicians "may choose whether to request a face-to-face appointment". The referring clinician fixes one thing the receiver cannot move, the Patient Indicated Date, which "cannot be changed due to capacity or access reasons".

## Two structures

The vertical structure is VHA's ordinary line: the Under Secretary for Health, the Assistant Under Secretary for Operations, VISN directors, facility directors and chiefs of staff, service line chiefs. The directive assigns each duties for oversight, training, resources and compliance. It also assigns roles that exist only for consults: Chiefs of Consult Referring Services and of Consult Receiving Services, referring and receiving clinicians, the Clinical Application Coordinator, the Referral Coordination Team, and the Chair of the facility's Consult Management Steering Committee, which "must assist" the director or chief of staff in managing and improving the facility's consult processes. A consult does not travel through this line, and a referring clinician has no authority over the receiving service's clinicians; the request reaches them as a request.

The horizontal structure is request and acceptance between services, which the directive grounds in the Care Coordination/Service Agreement: "a written agreement between two or more services within or between VA medical facilities, one of which sends a request for services to the other(s), defining the workflow rules and based on discussion and consensus". Service line chiefs sign it, and "consult processes across services should reflect" it. VISN directors are to ensure the use of VISN-level agreements and of agreements between facilities in the VISN. The agreement is a contract between units under one governance, written by the services themselves and held to the directive. Inter-facility consults extend the same structure across facilities, with agreements and coordination "needed between the VA medical facilities that will receive consults or send consults".

The two structures cross in the same people. A service chief is a line manager and a signatory of agreements; a clinician in a receiving service acts on others' requests and sends consults of her own. Nothing in the sources suggests one structure was drawn from the other. The line settles who must comply with the directive and what resources a service has. The agreements and the consult record settle what a service is asked for and how it answers.

## Authority and where facts live

Who may act on a consult is set locally. Anyone with CPRS access may originate one and anyone who can sign an order may sign it, but changing a consult's status needs update privileges, which the facility's ADPAC (the manual's term) grants service by service through a user-management option, along with the lists of people and teams notified. In the manual's words, tracking services "are not orderable unless the user is an update user for the service or its parent service". Facility-level rules for setting up consult services, stop codes and naming belong to the Clinical Application Coordinators, advised by the steering committee. The directive limits what the mechanism permits: cancelled consults may be resubmitted within 90 days, "may not be cancelled more than three times and may not be resubmitted more than two times", and auto-discontinue after the period a facility sets. Facilities must not use the group-update option to close consults "because of the risk of closing a consult without delivering needed care".

For inter-facility consults the software uses HL7 messages to carry requests and actions between "cooperating facilities". "Results are filed at the resulting facility", and CPRS reads them through Remote Data Views, so the requesting user need not treat the consult differently.

## Measures

The directive speaks in measures and indicators: "consult key performance indicators", the Stat consult to be completed "within 48 hours", pending status to be updated no later than 48 hours after receipt, with the remark that "Merely adding a comment without changing the status from Pending is not acceptable". Status is what the measures read, so the directive keeps it tied to the care it indicates. The Administrative Complete action (which "does not generate workload or an encounter") and the ban on group closure all concern a status changing without care being given. "Unable to Schedule" consults "are to remain open until the care is delivered".

## What the case shows

A request between services needs a record both can act on, statuses that separate the request's stages, and a rule about who may change them. The sources assign the role, the signature and the notification locally and make the national policy narrow: statuses, limits on resubmission, duties by role. The vocabulary fits the horizontal structure well, and strains at delegation: a consult is a service requested, with the receiving service accepting responsibility by its own act, so the authority and responsibility come through the directive and the agreements, not through a task handed down.
