# SAP ERP and the S/4HANA Universal Journal: deriving what used to be stored

An enterprise that runs its accounts payable, accounts receivable, general ledger, cost accounting and asset accounting on SAP software has one system integrating these functions over one database. The enterprise governs it: its finance and IT functions decide who may post, which accounting principles apply, and which custom programs run against the data. SAP is the supplier of the software and of its data model. Auditors, controllers and tax authorities rely on the postings, and the enterprise's own custom programs are clients of the data model, reading and writing tables that SAP defined. This case follows one change SAP made to that data model and what it cost clients. The case concerns a data model and database, and has no vertical or horizontal actor structure to show.

## The classic data model

Plattner describes the classic SAP Financials model in 2014. The actual accounting documents "are recorded in two tables as an accounting document header and its line items." Around them sit "tables that keep track of the total per account" (for example GLT0 for the general ledger and LFC1 for vendors) and tables that "replicate the accounting line items as materialized views with various filters and different sort orders to improve the performance of frequent queries". Open and closed items for vendors, customers and ledger accounts have their own tables, and controlling keeps its own document header and expense line items (COBK, COEP) with totals (COSP). His example is posting a vendor invoice: "fifteen consecutive steps", "ten inserts and five updates", where only the header and three line items are the accounting fact.

The reason was speed. Maintaining balances was "the only way we could achieve reasonable performance as calculating the balance on demand would require the expensive summation of all transfers." Plattner names three drawbacks of pre-built aggregates: "a lack of flexibility as they do not respond to organizational changes", "added complexity in enterprise applications" and "increased cost for data insertion as materialized aggregates have to be updated transactionally safe."

## The Universal Journal

In S/4HANA the Universal Journal (table ACDOCA) is where, in SAP Learning's words, the "actual financial accounting (FI) and controlling (CO) data is recorded in one single table". The page calls it the "definitive single source of truth for financial data". SAP Learning continues: "Since all financial data is based on the same line items, no reconciliation between financial accounting and controlling is necessary." It also describes the period-end work being retired: reconciling data between accounting and controlling because of differing detail, object types and design options. SAP PRESS (secondary, adapted from a book by Salmon and Haesendonckx) says data about one business transaction is "no longer chopped up and stored in different application tables but instead stored as a single, richer journal entry", with dimensions such as cost center, profit center and asset as fields of the line item. The ledger field separates documents kept under different accounting principles, and the posting logic is described as unchanged.

The enabling change was in the database, and Plattner's 2014 paper states the premise being given up: "the assumption that we can anticipate the right pre-aggregations for the majority of applications without creating a transactional bottleneck was completely wrong." With data held in memory in columns, "we could execute the queries of all business applications, including reporting, analytics, planning, etc. directly on the transactional line items." Some stored aggregation remains, below the application: a cache of intermediate results that "is maintained by the database and completely transparent to applications."

The paper reports measurements from productive use of what it calls the simplified SAP Financials, an application without transaction-maintained aggregates: vendor-invoice posting "2.5 times faster" than the classic transaction on a disk-based row store; the share of read queries in total execution time rising from 86 to 98 percent over a week of thousands of users' queries; and, in the classic application, "an average of 3.4 inserts or modifications to other tables within the same application module" per line item, against none in the simplified one. These are one author's figures from SAP's systems, and he advocated the approach: "I predict that all enterprise applications will be built in an aggregation and redundancy free manner in the future." The paper does not name ACDOCA or the Universal Journal. That its simplified application and the Universal Journal share one design is read here from the common premise of dropping stored aggregates.

## Clients that cannot change with the system

Custom programs and standard transactions read the old tables, and SAP kept them working. SAP Knowledge Base Article 3523400 says compatibility views are "required to map tables from the old world (e.g. COSS) to tables of the new S/4HANA world (e.g. ACDOCA) so that old coding can still work without changes." It adds, quoting SAP Note 2000002: "This mapping is typically rather complex, involving a lot of views and dependencies, so there is a significant risk that there are performance regressions compared to the former plain table access." The article lists performance regression, longer execution times and out-of-memory events among the issues, and calls the views "an abstraction layer over the actual database tables". SAP PRESS (secondary) says the index and totals tables are obsolete and "have been replaced by compatibility views to make sure that older custom programs will continue to work". A third-party German whitepaper (abat, secondary) says the views serve read access only and that tables such as COEP no longer exist as tables. SAP PRESS reports that BSEG, the classic line item table, stays relevant for operational processes such as open item management, with ACDOCA holding the full detail.

## Reading it

The clearest reading concerns source of truth and derivation. In the classic model a vendor balance could be authoritative in two places, the line items and the maintained total, and the financial-accounting and controlling document sets were two further copies of one transaction. Update steps inside every posting, and reconciliation at period end, kept them equal. Each was a redundant source of truth held together by work. The Universal Journal gives each posting one record and treats balances as derived. This is the heuristic that what can be derived is derived, and that storing a derived form is a performance choice with a consistency cost. The cost here was large, and the reason for paying it, that summing on disk was too slow, went away when the mechanism beneath, the database, changed. A family of mechanisms (totals tables, index tables, update steps, reconciliation) had grown around one premise about cost, and the premise was revised once, instead of each mechanism being trimmed.

SAP's phrase "single source of truth" holds within its scope: the journal is where the facts of a posting are authoritative for finance and controlling. It says nothing about copies elsewhere. SAP PRESS describes separate tables beside ACDOCA, ACDOCP for plan data and ACDOCU for consolidation, the latter "essentially an aggregation of the entries in the Universal Journal". The redundancy-free picture is the direction of the design, not a description of every table.

It is also a reformulation: the question of how to keep balances fast became the question of why store balances, and the sources present it as a changed assumption.

The compatibility views are a thin contract. SAP commits that old code works "without changes" and states the performance risk and out-of-memory side effects; it commits to no timing or service level. The views derive from ACDOCA, so they are a bridge and not a second source of truth.

The layer vocabulary fits modestly. Plattner's account moves the aggregate from the application to a cache in the database, so a responsibility changed layers while the SQL interface the applications use stayed. SAP's own "abstraction layer" for the views is a layer in this sense: it offers the old table shape and ACDOCA realizes it.

Most of this case needs no actor. Posting programs, views and the database are mechanisms, and the people who decide (controllers, auditors, SAP's product team) appear as stakeholders and requirement-setters. The case concerns the logical world and where derivation is placed.

## What it shows

Pre-computation responds to a cost, and the design that grows around it (totals, indexes, update choreography, reconciliation) depends on that cost remaining. When the cost changed, the larger gain was removing a family of mechanisms, not speeding them. The change also has a price when clients are many and old: a compatibility layer with its own failure modes. The sources are SAP's or its publisher's, and the case makes no judgment of adoption outcomes.
