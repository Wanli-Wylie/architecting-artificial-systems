# A balancing authority's balancing system

An interconnection has one frequency. NERC's reference document says each North American Interconnection "can be viewed as a single large machine with every generator pulling together to supply electricity to all customers", and calls holding its frequency near nominal "a fundamental indicator of the health of the power system". No one organization holds that quantity. The system studied here is one balancing authority's part of the work: the means by which it keeps generation, load and scheduled interchange in balance inside its area, and so contributes its share to frequency.

## The system and its governance

NERC's registry criteria define the balancing authority (BA) as "the responsible entity that integrates resource plans ahead of time, maintains Load-interchange-generation balance within a Balancing Authority Area, and supports Interconnection frequency in real-time". BA is a role that NERC registers organizations for, and the registered organization, with its system operators and dispatchers, fills it. The reference document says BAs "dispatch generating resources in order to meet their BAA demand", and some also control demand. The system's governance is the registered organization's own: its management, its operating procedures and its contracts with units. NERC's standards, the regulators who enforce them and the reliability coordinators, who "oversee the BA operations and coordination", lie beyond that boundary, as do neighbouring BAs. NERC and the regulators set obligations the system meets without holding their meaning, and the reliability coordinators watch over how it meets them. This is the case where governance is partial: the order that defines the role and its obligations is not the one that runs the system.

The generators it relies on hold roles that law, grid codes and contracts define, not this system. NERC registers generator owners and generator operators as functional entities, and the reference document lists regulation and frequency response as services that units provide. It also notes that capacity obligations have "historically been the purview of state and provincial regulatory bodies", so reserve and regulation arrangements differ across regions. How a BA contracts for regulation, and which units it assigns to AGC, is local practice.

## Mechanisms and loops

The balance is held by mechanisms with different speeds, which NERC calls a control continuum. Its summary table gives approximate timeframes: inertial control 0 to 12 seconds, primary control 10 to 60 seconds, secondary control 1 to 10 minutes, tertiary control 10 minutes to hours, time control hours.

Inertia comes first. NERC calls it "more of an effect than an actual control since it is governed by physical principles for most resources and emulated by others": rotating mass gives up energy as frequency falls, buying seconds for the rest. Governors come second, each a local device that senses frequency and adjusts its unit's output by a droop setting, with a typical droop of 5 percent and a deadband below which it ignores error. NERC likens them to "cruise controls for cars". They "operate independently from and much faster than system operator actions or those of AGC", and primary control "will not return frequency to nominal, but only arrest and stabilize it". Both are mechanisms. No one decides them in the moment, and a BA relies on them through the units it has.

Secondary control is the BA's own loop. Its energy management system (EMS) computes Reporting ACE from SCADA telemetry at least every six seconds: (NIA – NIS) – 10B (FA – FS) – IME, the gap between actual and scheduled net interchange, less a frequency term sized by the BA's bias setting B, less a metering-error correction. NERC says ACE "is to a BA what frequency is to the Interconnection". Automatic generation control (AGC) "generally sends signals that cause resources performing secondary control to move to oppose the ACE", as raise/lower pulses or MW set points. AGC is a fixed law over ACE and set points, so it is a mechanism too, and operators can feed it a control ACE that differs from reporting ACE, for instance to pay down accumulated inadvertent energy. Dispatchers add manual adjustments, and tertiary control deploys and then restores reserves. Here the vocabulary's actor description fits: the dispatchers choose among options the controllers leave open, such as which resources to move, when to correct ACE and how to restore reserves.

The bias term connects the loops. Frequency response is what inertia and governors actually deliver across the BA's area, and bias is its approximation, "designed to prevent AGC withdrawal of frequency support following a disturbance". If the two were equal, ACE would not move when the BA's governors respond. NERC's worked example has a BA at 60.01 Hz that should still raise generation by 5 MW, because its interchange shortfall exceeds its bias obligation. The slower loop is built so as not to undo the faster one's work.

## Requirements from beyond the boundary

NERC's standards state what the system must achieve, in measures. BAL-001-2 has the purpose "To control Interconnection frequency within defined limits": R1 asks for CPS1 of at least 100 percent over twelve months, and R2 for clock-minute average ACE not to exceed its limit (BAAL) for more than 30 consecutive minutes. NERC describes BAAL in these words: "In simple terms, BAAL assigns each BA a share of the responsibility for control of steady-state Interconnection frequency." BAL-003-2 requires an annual Frequency Response Measure at least as negative as the BA's Frequency Response Obligation, and sets rules for the bias setting in ACE. The BA meets these requirements without holding their meaning: the standards, and their revision, belong to NERC and the regulators.

These measures are both requirements and indicators, and the reference document is open about what they miss. CPS1 is measured at one averaging window, so "there is no assurance that frequency variation will be constrained at other averaging points". Its benchmark was set to "bound acceptable performance but not raise the bar". The earlier ten-minute measure CPS2 was replaced because it "was not dependent on ACE’s impact on frequency" and "did not provide the correct signal for maintaining frequency"; the frequency-sensitive BAAL took its place. On time error correction, NERC writes that "nobody can say with assurance who or what would be impacted" if the practice were halted.

## Horizontal relations and the interchange meter

Inside the BA, operators and the EMS act on units through dispatch and AGC signals. Its main relations with others are horizontal. Neighbouring BAs are joined by tie-lines and exchange scheduled interchange, and at each boundary "there will be an agreed upon meter" that both neighbours use "to perform balancing operations and accounting". The metered flow is one fact with one value for both sides, and the IME term exists because instantaneous meters "are not always as accurate as the hourly meters". The standards also provide for agreements among BAs: BAL-001-2 names Overlap Regulation Service and Regulation Reserve Sharing Groups, and BAL-003-2 names Frequency Response Sharing Groups, which can take over a BA's responsibility for a measure. These are shared rules and agreements among independent organizations, and they are part of this system's environment rather than of its design.

## What the case shows

A quantity that cannot be held at one place can be held by controls with different speeds, each acting on its own signal, with the signal handed upward built so that the faster mechanism's work survives. Physical processes and fixed control laws carry the first seconds, and deciding people only the later minutes, and a design that treated everything as an actor would hide that. The requirement is a bound on one participant's contribution to a shared physical quantity, stated as measures that were revised when they stopped pointing at it.

Where the vocabulary strains: the obligations are tasks assigned to a role by standard, and they are not delegation, since no higher actor hands a BA a task and awaits a result. NERC speaks of a continuum with overlapping timeframes, so "layer" fits as a description of speed and not as a sharp stack. Composition rule applies, if at all, to the statistical rule of CPS1, since frequency is a physical aggregate and no rule derives it from parts. State estimation does not belong to this loop: ACE comes from direct tie-line, frequency and schedule telemetry.
